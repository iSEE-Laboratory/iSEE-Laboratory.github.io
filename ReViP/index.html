Overview

Visual feedback inside action generation.

A VLA may keep acting on presumed task progress even after the scene has invalidated it. A dropped object, for example, can remain on the floor while the policy continues its placement motion.

ReViP connects visual stage verification to action revision, closing the loop between visual feedback and action generation. Visual-Progress Grounding integrates verified stage cues into policy representations. Stage-Aware Attention Rebalancing uses those cues and execution context to guide action generation.

Motivation

“False Completion” in VLA Models

VLAs can proceed as if a task stage were complete despite visible failure. Below, π0 continues placement after the object drops.

Our key insight. Task progress must remain grounded in current visual evidence, and that grounding must influence subsequent actions.

Methods

Visual-Progress Rebalancing

When execution cues dominate presumed task progress, updated images may not revise the next action. ReViP verifies task-stage conditions from current observations and uses the resulting cues to guide both policy representations and action-generating attention.

Visual-Progress Grounding

A VLM observer reasons about task-stage conditions to extract cues describing the valid stage and immediate intention. FiLM integrates these cues into the policy’s vision–language representation.

Stage-Aware Attention Rebalancing

Stage cues and robot state guide group-wise attention adjustments. Dual calibration regulates their strength and token-wise allocation, emphasizing stage-relevant visual regions while retaining proprioceptive information for manipulation.

ReViP adds less than 0.4% trainable parameters to the base policy.

ReViP-Flash

Cue distillation trains an in-policy observer to predict stage cues from native features and visual changes. This removes external VLM inference at deployment while retaining both ReViP modules.

Experiments

Experimental Results

1. Real-World Results

Object placement and drawer manipulation evaluate recovery, target selection, and multi-stage execution under visual disturbances.

2. Simulation Results

Multiple Benchmarks

ReViP improves performance on the False-Completion Benchmark, LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and four RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, covering visual-progress conflicts, general manipulation, distribution shifts, and bimanual manipulation.

Multiple VLA Backbones

Consistent gains on π0, OpenVLA-OFT, and GR00T N1.5 demonstrate applicability across flow-matching, autoregressive, and dual-system architectures.
